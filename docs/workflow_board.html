Four apps, one workflow, one optional listening tool

How to sequence Capability Compass, Org Modeling, Process Map Studio and the AI Org Advisory Workbench to assess and design a fit-for-purpose operating model and system, and when to add Narrative Lens (TESSA) to hear the shop floor. Start with the current-state analysis; the rest of the journey follows from it.

Your immediate need. Nine steps that frame the analysis, gather the evidence in each country, and end with six pages for EXCO, plus one optional step for hearing the shop floor. Click a step for exactly where to go and what "done" looks like. Ticks are remembered on this computer. New to multi-unit work? Read Frame once, score each unit first. Where an app says "country", type whatever unit you are comparing: a country, a station or an asset.

What good looks like at the end of the current-state analysis. Seven mock-up screens of the outcomes you are working towards, and what each one hands to the design phase. All names and numbers are illustrative and fictional (a made-up group with three made-up countries: Arden, Belmar and Caldera).

The whole journey: six stages, four apps and one optional. Each card is what one app does in one stage. Click a card for the question it answers and what it passes on. Empty cells mean that app has no part in that stage. Narrative Lens is optional: use it when you need it, skip it when you do not.

Narrative Lens · optional

Not connected by file: it comes in as evidence

Narrative Lens shares no IDs and exchanges no files with the other four. Download its Pattern Brief (and the stories CSV if needed) and upload it under Compass → Evidence in the workspace of the country it describes. Cite it on the cells it bears on, usually Culture and Process, and grade it Indicated: stories show where friction is felt, not why.

Required

Chosen

Three rules that keep the comparison honest

The home workspace is also a unitIt describes the whole organisation in its charter, but you score it as one unit, like the others. There is no separate, unscored "group" workspace above the units. Choose as home the unit you know best and have the most evidence for.
One kind of unit per comparisonPick one level: countries, or stations, or assets, or business units. Comparing a region with a single asset makes every difference meaningless. The Compass does not add units up into a regional total; if you need a region-level view later, set it up as a separate comparison.
Frame changes flow one way, and only addChange the frame in the home workspace, then open each unit and use Review frame changes. Only additions and renames come in, ticked one by one. Nothing is deleted, and no score, status, weight or amount moves.

What is set once, and what each unit fills in

Set once, in the frame (home workspace)

  • Charter: organisation name, sponsor, thesis, scope, design criteria, decision owners
  • The frame key (each unit's key is built from it)
  • Clusters and their IDs, scope (Scored, Enabler, Context only) and diagnostic question
  • The list of money levers and the list of anchor roles
  • The unit word: country, station, asset or business unit
  • The period and the volume measure for unit economics

Filled in by each unit

  • Evidence, scores, evidence grades and rationale
  • Whether each anchor role exists, is thin or is absent there (they start as "To confirm")
  • Money weights and amounts in that unit's currency
  • Hand-off decisions and the value stream to examine (choose the same one across units)
  • Revenue, manpower and non-manpower cost, head-count and volume (unit economics)
  • Its SOP register: owner role, who updates each SOP, review dates
  • The process owner, steps and handoffs for each value stream it maps
  • Quick-win candidates, findings from the other apps, saved versions

Before you add any units: lock the frame

Each unit is a copy of the frame as it stands. Getting these right first saves a round of frame reviews in every unit. Ticks are remembered on this computer.

    Questions worth asking before you start

    Can I add a unit later?

    Yes. Add it from Charter → Units to compare at any time. It is copied from the frame as it stands that day, so it starts up to date.

    A cluster does not apply in one unit. Do I delete it there?

    No. Deleting breaks the line-up with the other units. Leave its cells Not scored and write why in the cell notes. If it applies nowhere this cycle, set it to Context only in the home frame and bring that change into each unit.

    Who should score each unit?

    Ideally the unit lead, from that unit's own evidence. Open the unit, Export, send the file, and Import what comes back. Different people score differently, so before EXCO have the leads look at each other's evidence on the widest gaps.

    Where does the comparison happen, and what does it need?

    On Current State → Compare countries, in any workspace of the frame. Every unit must be in the Compass on the same computer and browser, so import the files the unit leads send back. Each unit needs its scores and a saved version that holds them.

    How do I avoid losing work?

    The Compass keeps everything in this browser only. Clearing browser data, or switching computer, loses it. Export each workspace at the end of every working session and keep the files in your approved storage.

    Can I change the frame key after adding units?

    Avoid it. Every unit's key, and so every ID quoted in a board paper, is built from it. Settle the key before adding the first unit.

    What about confidential documents?

    Workspace files and exports hold your organisation's data: keep them where your policy allows. Analyse and synthesise evidence sends document text to the Claude API, so check your data policy before using it on restricted documents. You can always score by hand and cite the document without sending it.

    When should I save a version?

    In each unit, once its cells are scored, and again after findings come back and you re-score. A version with no scores is ignored, and the app warns before saving one.

    Can I show EXCO an early read?

    Yes. The readout assembles from whatever is scored and imported. Printed at about week 6, it is your early read; pages with thin evidence say so rather than filling the gap. Quick-win candidates appear on page 6 as "Act now".

    What EXCO sees. The current-state analysis ends in six pages, printed from Compass → Current State → EXCO readout. You write two things: the verdict and the ask. Everything else is counted or quoted, so every line traces to a score or a finding.

    Before you present

    • Every in-scope cluster scored in every country. An unscored cell reads "Not scored" and is never counted as weak; context-only clusters are left out by a stated reason.
    • Every material difference (two levels or more) classed Required, Chosen or Inherited, with a one-line reason.
    • Findings imported for each country, or pages 3 to 5 will say plainly that they are empty.
    • Country leads have looked at each other's evidence for the widest gaps. Different people score different countries, and one may simply be stricter.
    • If you ran Narrative Lens, its patterns are cited as Indicated evidence behind a score. They are never a finding on their own.
    • Money at risk is an estimate until finance validates it, and is only totalled when the countries share a currency.
    • Unit economics entered for every unit, on the same period, head-count basis and volume measure, and validated by Finance (or shown as an estimate). Org Modeling's people cost is base salary only.
    • Every decision finding says who is accountable, not only who decides.
    • Every mapped unit lists its SOPs with an owner role and a review date; overdue and uncontrolled copies are flagged on page 5.
    • Every value stream has a process owner named in each unit, and steps and handoffs for every mapped unit.
