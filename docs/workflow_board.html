Four apps, one workflow, one optional listening tool

How to sequence Capability Compass, Org Modeling, Process Map Studio and the AI Org Advisory Workbench to assess and design a fit-for-purpose operating model and system, and when to add Narrative Lens (TESSA) to hear the shop floor. Start with the current-state analysis; the rest of the journey follows from it.

Your immediate need. Nine steps that frame the analysis, gather the evidence in each country, and end with six pages for EXCO, plus one optional step for hearing the shop floor. Click a step for exactly where to go and what "done" looks like. Ticks are remembered on this computer. Where an app says "country", type whatever unit you are comparing: a country, a station or an asset.

What good looks like at the end of the current-state analysis. Seven mock-up screens of the outcomes you are working towards, and what each one hands to the design phase. All names and numbers are illustrative and fictional (a made-up group with three made-up countries: Arden, Belmar and Caldera).

The whole journey: six stages, four apps and one optional. Each card is what one app does in one stage. Click a card for the question it answers and what it passes on. Empty cells mean that app has no part in that stage. Narrative Lens is optional: use it when you need it, skip it when you do not.

What travels between the apps. Two small files and one shared language. Nothing is retyped, and every ID means the same thing in every app and in every country.

Narrative Lens · optional

Not connected by file: it comes in as evidence

Narrative Lens shares no IDs and exchanges no files with the other four. Download its Pattern Brief (and the stories CSV if needed) and upload it under Compass → Evidence in the workspace of the country it describes. Cite it on the cells it bears on, usually Culture and Process, and grade it Indicated: stories show where friction is felt, not why.

Why a difference between countries exists: three words, used in every app

Required

Regulation, a customer contract or the physical site makes it so.→ Leave local

Chosen

A deliberate local decision, made for a reason someone can state.→ Decide: keep or align

Inherited

History, an acquisition or habit. Nobody decided it.→ Harmonise

Each country has its own key in front (for example a frame key plus a two-letter country code), so an ID quoted in a board paper always says which country it came from.

What EXCO sees. The current-state analysis ends in six pages, printed from Compass → Current State → EXCO readout. You write two things: the verdict and the ask. Everything else is counted or quoted, so every line traces to a score or a finding.

Before you present

  • Every country fully scored. An unscored cell reads "Not scored"; it is never counted as weak.
  • Every material difference (two levels or more) classed Required, Chosen or Inherited, with a one-line reason.
  • Findings imported for each country, or pages 3 to 5 will say plainly that they are empty.
  • Country leads have looked at each other's evidence for the widest gaps. Different people score different countries, and one may simply be stricter.
  • If you ran Narrative Lens, its patterns are cited as Indicated evidence behind a score. They are never a finding on their own.
  • Money at risk is an estimate until finance validates it, and is only totalled when the countries share a currency.
